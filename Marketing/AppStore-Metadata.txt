3. DESCRIPTION (4000 max)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

openWater is a GPS speed and technique tracker for wingfoiling, parawinging,
downwinding, windsurfing, kitesurfing and sailing. It is open source, works
entirely offline, and never asks you to create an account.

Everything in it is free. No subscription, no trial, no locked features — the
full speed categories, foiling analysis, gybe and tack analysis, distance
splits, session replay and the race countdown are all just part of the app.

RECORD ON YOUR WATCH OR YOUR PHONE

Start a session in one tap. The Apple Watch app runs standalone — it does not
need your phone nearby, or even installed. Water Lock engages automatically,
and the session keeps recording with the screen off and your wrist down. Your
track is written to disk continuously, so a flat battery or a crash costs you
seconds, not your session.

Seven live screens: speed, splits, session totals, wind angles, foil time,
controls, and a racing start countdown you can sync to the committee boat's gun.

SPEED, MEASURED PROPERLY

Every category the speedsurfing community actually uses:

• 2 second peak
• Best 10 seconds, and the average of your 5 best 10-second runs
• 100 m, 250 m, 500 m, 1 km, 2 km, nautical mile, 5 km, 10 km
• Alpha 500 — a 500 m run with a gybe, finishing within 50 m of its start
• 30 minute and 1 hour averages
• Any custom distance you add, applied retroactively to sessions you already
  recorded

These are computed on the continuous track rather than snapped to the nearest
GPS fix, because a single second of slop is ten metres at twenty knots. Speed
comes from the receiver's Doppler measurement, not from differencing positions.

SEE WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

An hour of winging in a bay is forty passes through the same water, and drawn
as one line it is unreadable. openWater fixes that:

• Flights are drawn bold, slow sections fade back, falls get a marker
• Pick any single run and the other thirty-nine drop away
• The Runs view drops the map entirely — one lane per run, stacked in time, so
  nothing overlaps. It reads top to bottom like a score.
• Replay the whole session with a playhead, speed heatmap and live readout

FOR FOILERS

• Time on foil, number of flights, longest flight, takeoff speed
• How long your pump-up takes
• Falls counted separately from touchdowns, because they are not the same thing
• Longest clean run — the metric that keeps improving long after top speed stops

FOR WING AND PARAWING

• Wind direction worked out from your own track, with no weather service and no
  internet needed
• Polar curve, VMG upwind and downwind, tacking and gybing angles
• Gybes and tacks detected and scored: entry speed, minimum speed, recovery
  time, and whether you stayed on the foil
• Port versus starboard comparison, so you can see the side you have been
  quietly avoiding

FOR DOWNWINDING

• Glides detected from the pump-and-glide rhythm
• Glide count, longest glide, and what share of the run was free
• How often you linked to the next glide without dropping
• The swell period you were riding

YOUR DATA IS YOURS

• Import GPX, TCX, FIT and CSV — Garmin, Coros, Suunto and most apps
• Export GPX, TCX, CSV, GeoJSON, or a complete lossless archive
• No account, no server, no tracking. Nothing leaves your device unless you
  send it.
• Sharing trims the start and end of your track by default, because those are
  usually your car or your home

HONEST NUMBERS

Every session is graded for GPS quality. A figure computed without Doppler
speed says so. A detected gybe or jump carries a confidence, and the raw
samples are always kept so you can check anything. openWater will tell you when
it does not know something rather than guessing.

OPEN SOURCE

The complete source, including every analysis algorithm, is public. If you
disagree with how a number is calculated, you can read exactly what it does —
and change it.

5. WHAT'S NEW (4000 max) — version 1.2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Spots is now a map you can read. The wind wash draws as one picture,
    with a model picker and an hour clock you can turn three days ahead.
    Tides and currents get their own screens, in the nearest station's own
    numbers. The radar loops smoothly and folds its controls away. Saved
    routes and private spots, a centre pin that says what the water is
    doing, and a forecast that says so when a request missed rather than
    showing you an old one.

    Wave rides. Point the swell arrow and see every wave you caught on the
    map, watch them go past in order, and decide for yourself what counts
    as a ride. Waves caught straight off the back of the last one are found
    and marked linked. On a downwinder, one tap takes the wind as the swell.

    Upwind. Tap one leg and then another to measure the stretch between
    them the same honest way the best beat is measured. Point the VMG at
    where you were actually going — up the river, not into the bank.

    Apple TV. The whole conditions picture on the big screen: wind, wash,
    radar, tide and the cameras, for the spots you have saved.

    Apple Watch shows two screens unless you ask for the rest, and pressing
    stop always puts the session somewhere — or says why not.

    And the small things: the screens agree with each other, a session is
    never lost quietly, the map takes the whole screen when you turn the
    phone, and a bug button in the corner sends your numbers with a note.

9. APP PRIVACY ("nutrition label")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Answer: "Data Is Collected" — one category only, and only when the user asks
for it.

    DATA TYPE:        Location — Precise Location
    LINKED TO USER:   No
    USED FOR TRACKING: No
    PURPOSE:          App Functionality

    Declare this because of two features, both opt-in per use:

    "Share a Link". When a rider taps it, openWater uploads a reduced copy of
    that session — the track, the speeds and the splits — to Firebase Storage
    and returns a link they can send to anyone.

    "Send feedback" (the bug button on every session screen). Sends the
    rider's note and the numbers the screen was showing to Firestore. Only if
    the rider switches on "Send my recording too" does the session archive —
    the full track — go with it. So the label also needs:

    DATA TYPE:        User Content — Other User Content (the note)
    LINKED TO USER:   No
    USED FOR TRACKING: No
    PURPOSE:          App Functionality

    DATA TYPE:        Contact Info — Email Address (optional, if the rider
                      types one into the feedback sheet's contact field)
    LINKED TO USER:   No
    USED FOR TRACKING: No
    PURPOSE:          App Functionality

    Nothing is uploaded until the rider taps one of those two. The rest of
    the app's network use is reading, not collecting: Spots fetches forecasts,
    tides and buoys from Open-Meteo, NOAA and NDBC, and the spot guide and
    wind-station registry from Firebase, for the place on the map. Those
    requests carry a coordinate and no identifier, and the app keeps nothing
    server-side from them.

    The label as of the 1.0 submission declared Precise Location for "Share a
    Link" only. Update it in App Store Connect for 1.2 — the label is edited
    there, separately from the build.

    Everything else remains true and is checkable in the source:

    • No analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier
    • No account, no sign-in, no identifier of any kind attached to a share
    • Recording, analysis, import and export all work with no network at all
    • Location, heart rate and motion never leave the device otherwise
    • HealthKit is written to only, and only with the user's permission

    What a share does NOT include: notes, gear, heart rate, the device model,
    the raw GPS fixes, or any identifier. The first and last 200 m of the track
    are trimmed by default so a shared map does not show the launch point.

    Because there is no account, a share cannot be listed or deleted from
    inside the app — the share screen says so before the upload, not after. If
    that becomes a data-deletion problem under App Review's rules, the answer is
    a per-share delete token, not silence.

    If an analytics SDK or any further server feature is ever added, this
    section must be updated before that build ships.

Permission strings shipping in the build:

    NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription
        openWater records your GPS track so it can measure your speed,
        distance and runs.

    NSLocationAlwaysAndWhenInUseUsageDescription
        openWater records your GPS track while you are on the water, including
        when your phone is in a pocket with the screen off.

    NSMotionUsageDescription
        openWater uses the motion sensors to tell when you are flying on the

    NSHealthShareUsageDescription (watch)

    NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription (watch)

10. REVIEW NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

    openWater is a GPS speed tracker for watersports. No account is required
    and there is nothing to sign in to.

    TO TEST WITHOUT GOING ON THE WATER
    The app ships with a demo session generator. On the Sessions tab, tap the
    + button in the top right and choose "Add Demo Session". This creates a
    complete wingfoil session and runs the full analysis on it, so every screen
    — map, runs, charts, records, trends and session replay — can be reviewed
    without recording anything.

    LOCATION USE
    Location is used only while a session is recording, which the user starts
    explicitly with the Record button. The "always" authorization and the
    location background mode are needed because a session runs for one to three
    hours with the phone in a pocket or a waterproof pouch and the screen off;
    without background location the recording stops and the user loses their
    session. Location data is transmitted only when the user taps "Share a
    Link" on a finished session, or switches on "Send my recording too" in
    the feedback sheet — see below.

    SHARING A SESSION TO THE WEB
    Session detail → ••• → "Share a Link" uploads a reduced copy of that one
    session (track, speeds, splits) to Firebase Storage and returns a link at
    openwaterapp.com/s/<code>. It never happens automatically, and the screen
    states exactly what is and is not included before the upload. There is no
    account: the 22-character code in the link is what keeps a share unlisted.

    SENDING FEEDBACK
    The ladybird button on every session screen opens a sheet where the user
    can write a note about what the app got wrong. Sending it uploads the note
    and the numbers that screen was showing. A separate switch, off by
    default, attaches the session recording. Nothing else in the app writes to
    a server; Spots reads forecasts, tides and buoys from Open-Meteo and NOAA
    and the spot guide from Firebase, credited on their own screens.

    APPLE WATCH
    The watch app records independently and does not require the iPhone to be
    present. Completed sessions transfer to the iPhone when it is next
    reachable.

    HEALTHKIT
    Used on the watch to keep the workout session alive (which is what allows
    background GPS on watchOS), to read heart rate, and to save the finished
    workout with its route. Declining Health permission does not prevent
    recording.

    OPEN SOURCE
    Full source: https://github.com/laanlabs/openwater             [VERIFY]

    WEATHERKIT / APPLE WEATHER (Guideline 5.2.5)
    The app uses WeatherKit in two places, each showing the " Weather"
    mark linked to https://weatherkit.apple.com/legal-attribution.html:
    1. Saving or editing a session → "Use recorded conditions" — fills the
       session's wind from Apple Weather's hourly history for that time and
       place; the attribution sits directly under the result.
    2. Settings → Weather — a live service-status reading.
    A screen recording from a physical device showing both is attached per
    the review request of Aug 14, 2026 (submission de7fc7ff). The wind on
    the Record tab and everything in Spots (forecasts, buoys, tides) comes
    from non-Apple sources (Open-Meteo, NOAA/NDBC), credited on their own
    screens.

    All three iPhone sizes are provided because App Store Connect's required
    size depends on which display slot the app record uses, and it rejects
    anything that is not an exact match. Upload whichever it asks for.

    The watch screenshots are plain captures rather than composed panels:
    a watch screenshot is 410 points wide on the listing, and headline text
    composited at that size is illegible. They were captured from a genuine
    recording session — the simulator was driven along a real route with
    `simctl location`, so the speeds, splits and flight timer on them were
    all actually computed.

    The Apple TV screenshots are captures at native 3840 x 2160, which is
    one of the two sizes App Store Connect accepts for that platform (the
    other is 1920 x 1080). No frame and no composed headline: a television
    screenshot needs no device frame because the screen is the product, and
    the listing is read on the same television at the same distance, so the
    app's own type is already the right size.

    They are captured with Marketing/capture-tv-screenshots.sh, which seeds
    a known set of saved spots first — otherwise the favourites screen
    photographs whatever was last left on the device.

    Order: the map the app opens on, the full report under its crosshairs,
    the five-day model comparison, the camera grid, the cameras on a map,
    the radar, and the saved spots board.

    1. Every speed category, measured properly
    2. See what actually happened  (state-aware map)
    3. Runs, unstacked              (Ribbon)

    5. Angles, gybes and time on foil
    6. Know before you drive        (Spots)
    7. Tools for the beach
    8. One tap and you're riding    (Record)
